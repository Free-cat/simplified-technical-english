# STE dictionary: working subset

This file is **not** the official ASD-STE100 dictionary. It is a working subset of
the cases that occur most frequently. The official dictionary has about 900
approved words and many more unapproved entries. ASD supplies it free of charge on
request (asd-ste100.org). If the user has the official PDF or a company term base,
that source is the authority. If a word is not in this file and you are not sure,
prefer a word that you know is approved, or say that the word must be confirmed.

## 1. How the dictionary works

- An approved word has **one part of speech** and **one meaning**. In the official
  dictionary, approved words are in CAPITAL letters and unapproved words in lower
  case. This file uses the same notation. The capitals are a notation only: in a
  document, write the words in normal case ("must", not "MUST").
- An unapproved entry gives one or more approved alternatives. The alternative
  sometimes has a different part of speech, and then you must change the sentence
  construction (rule 9.1).
- A word that is not in the dictionary at all is permitted only as a technical
  noun or a technical verb (sections 8 and 9 below).

## 3. Unapproved modal and helping verbs

| Do not use | Use |
|---|---|
| should, shall, ought to, have to, need to, is to / are to | MUST (obligation); RECOMMEND (advice) |
| may, might, could | CAN (capability, permission); "it is possible that" / POSSIBLY (possibility) |
| would | Rewrite with the simple present or simple future |
| has / have / had + participle | Simple past, or BE + adjective |
| is / are + -ing | Simple present |

| DAMAGE | noun | verb | cause damage to |

## 8. Technical noun categories

A word that is not in the dictionary is a technical noun if it names something in
one of these categories. (The official standard lists the categories with examples;
this is a condensed list.)

1. Names in official parts information: *bolt, gasket, O-ring, circuit breaker*
2. Vehicles, machines, and locations on them: *aircraft, forklift, cockpit, engine bay*
3. Tools and support equipment: *wrench, torque wrench, multimeter, jack*
4. Materials, consumables, and unwanted material: *grease, sealant, lockwire, debris*
5. Facilities and infrastructure: *hangar, workshop, server room, runway*
6. Systems, components, circuits, and their functions: *hydraulic system, relay, bus*
7. Mathematical, scientific, and engineering terms: *diameter, voltage, torque, ratio*
8. Navigation and geographic terms: *north, latitude, heading*
9. Numbers, units of measurement, and time: *25, psi, mm, second*
10. Quoted text: placards, labels, signs, messages, UI text: *"LOW PRESSURE", Save*
11. Persons, groups, and organizations: *technician, operator, administrator, crew*
12. Parts of the body: *hand, eye, skin*
13. Common personal effects and food: *watch, glasses, water*
14. Medical terms: *burn, dermatitis*
15. Official documents and their parts: *manual, checklist, table, figure, step*
16. Environmental and operational conditions: *humidity, turbulence, overload*
17. Colors: *red, amber*
18. Damage terms: *crack, corrosion, dent, scratch*
19. Information technology and telephony terms: *server, database, token, password, file*
20. Civil or military operations terms: *mission, patrol, air traffic control*

Test: can you point to the thing, measure it, or find it in a parts list, a
glossary, or a standard of the field? If yes, it is probably a TN. A general word
used loosely ("the functionality", "the solution", "the approach") is not a TN.

## 9. Technical verb categories

1. Manufacturing processes
   - Remove material: *drill, grind, mill, ream, sand, machine*
   - Add material: *insulate, plate, coat, paint, prime*
   - Attach material: *bolt, bond, braze, crimp, rivet, solder, weld*
   - Change the mechanical properties: *anneal, cure, heat-treat, magnetize*
   - Change the surface: *buff, polish, burnish*
   - Change the shape: *cast, extrude, forge, stamp*
2. Computer processes and applications: *click, enter, type, scroll, download,
   upload, save, delete, log in, encrypt, compile, deploy, reboot, back up*
3. Descriptions and operational language: *taxi, land, arm, dock, ground (electrical)*

A general verb is not a technical verb. "Verify", "perform", and "utilize" are
not processes of a subject field; they are general English, and the dictionary
has approved alternatives.

## 10. Software and IT documentation

STE started in aerospace, but it is used for software, medical devices, and
machinery. For software text:

- UI labels are quoted text (category 10). Write them exactly as on the screen,
  with the user's markup convention: **Save**, `Settings > Security`.
- Software objects are technical nouns (category 19): *repository, branch,
  endpoint, API key, container, queue, dashboard, snapshot, migration, rollback,
  release tag, log*. Use one name for one object.
- Software operations are technical verbs (category 2) when the verb names a
  specific operation that the reader does with a tool: *click, select, enter,
  install, deploy, restart, merge, commit, roll back, run* (a script or a
  command), *drop* (a table), *drain* (a queue), *scale* (a pool), *trigger*
  (a job), *upgrade, migrate, log in, back up*. Use the verb that the tool or
  the team uses. Do not replace it with a general verb that loses the meaning.
- The same word in a general sense is not a technical verb. "Run the script" is
  correct. "The service runs on the cluster" is general: write "The service
  operates on the cluster".
- General verbs still follow the dictionary: "Make sure that the service
  operates" (not "Verify the service is running"), "Use the `--force` flag"
  (not "Leverage").
- *Deploy* is a verb. "Do a deployment" is also possible if *deployment* is a
  technical noun in the project. Do not mix the two for the same action.
- "Turn on / turn off / enable / disable" a setting: write "Set <the setting> to
  ON / OFF", or use the label of the control ("Select **Maintenance mode**").
- Code, commands, paths, and log text are not prose. Do not change them. Each
  code span counts as one word.
- Words to delete or replace: *leverage, robust, seamless, simply, just, please,
  straightforward, feature-rich, in order to, as needed, if applicable*.
  "Please" is not approved. Give the command directly.
