## 1. Words

- 1.2 — Not STE: "Test the circuit." (*test* is a noun here.) STE: "Do a test of the circuit."
- 1.2 — Not STE: "Oil the bearing." (*oil* is a TN.) STE: "Apply oil to the bearing."
- 1.3 — Not STE: "Follow the aircraft" when you mean "obey". *Follow* means "come after". STE: "Obey the safety instructions."
- 1.3 — "Wear gloves" → "Put on gloves" (*wear* means "become damaged by friction"). "More than 20 psi", not "above 20 psi" (*above* is for positions).
- 1.4 — Use only the verb and adjective forms that the dictionary gives.
- 1.6 — "Do not go close to the engine" → "Do not go near the engine." "Close-tolerance bolt" can be a TN when it is the approved part name. "Main" is not approved ("primary"), but "main landing gear" is a TN.
- 1.7 — A word can be a TN and a TV if it fits a category of each type: "a carbide drill" (tool) and "Drill a hole" (process).
- 1.10 — "Do not brick the router" and "Remove your gear" are jargon.
- 1.11 — If the first mention is "access panel", do not write "cover", "door", or "hatch" for the same item later.
- 1.12 — "If you detect broken wires" → "If you find damaged wires." But "The scanner detects metal objects" is correct (a technical verb for a function of equipment).
- 1.13 — Not STE: "Give the panel a sand." STE: "Sand the panel." The past participle as an adjective is permitted: "the sanded panel".
- 1.14 — Do not change British spelling in quoted text.

## 2. Multi-word nouns

- 2.1 — Not STE: "engine fuel pump inlet filter element". STE: "the filter element at the inlet of the engine fuel pump".
- 2.2 — "heavy-duty hoist drive belt (the belt)". After that, use "the belt".
- An article does not count. A hyphenated group counts as one word: "cutoff-switch power connection" has three words.
- Do not put hyphens between all the words. Do not add hyphens to a name of three words or fewer, and do not remove a hyphen from an approved name.
- Do not replace a short name with abbreviations ("the DA", "the PVA").

## 3. Verbs and modal verbs

- 3.2/3.4 — "The pump has failed" → "The pump is unserviceable." (*Fail* is not an approved verb.)
- 3.4 — "The engine is running" → "The engine operates" or "The engine is in operation".
- 3.3 — "The valve is closed" is correct (an adjective of state). "The valve was closed by the technician" → "The technician closed the valve."
- 3.5 — "Before removing the cover" → "Before you remove the cover". But "the operating temperature", "the wiring", and "a bearing" are TNs. An *-ing* word is also permitted as a title or heading that is a TN: "Filtering", "Rinsing", "Labeling".
- 3.5 — "Using a wrench, tighten the nut" → "Tighten the nut with a wrench."
- 3.6 — "The bolts are to be torqued" → "Torque the bolts to 25 Nm."
- 3.6 — To change the passive: make the agent the subject ("A relay connects the circuits"); use the command form in a procedure; use "you" for the reader or "we" for the company. If the agent is unknown, "The data was corrupted" is permitted in a description.
- 3.7 — "Perform the installation of the unit" → "Install the unit."
- Modal verbs: CAN, MUST, and WILL (simple future) are approved. Should, may, might, would, shall, ought to, need to, and have to are not. Use MUST for an obligation, CAN for a capability or permission, "it is possible that" or POSSIBLY for a possibility, and "We recommend that" for advice. In a procedure, do not put "must" before a command (rule 5.3).

## 4. Sentences

- 4.2 — "Check valve closed" → "Make sure that the valve is closed." "Don't" → "Do not".
- 4.2 — "Can be a maximum of 5 mm" → "The gap can be a maximum of 5 mm."
- 4.1 — "No leaks are permitted" → "Make sure that there are no leaks."
- 4.2 — Do not omit the subject: "If installed, remove the shims" → "If shims are installed, remove them."
- 4.3 — A list has a colon after the lead-in, a capital letter at the start of each item, and no comma or semicolon at the end of an item. Put a period after the last item. Do not mix instructions and descriptions in one list. In a safety instruction, write "Do not" in each item.
- 4.4 — Connecting words: and, but, then, thus, as a result, also, because, if, when, while, after, before, until, unless. Example: "The pressure decreases. As a result, the valve closes."
- 4.5 — "Remove cover from pump" → "Remove the cover from the pump." No article in a general statement ("Solvents can cause damage to paint"), and none before a name with an identifier ("Tag circuit breaker 36L7").

## 5. Procedures

- 5.2 — "Remove the four bolts and lift the cover and put it on the bench" → three steps.
- 5.2 — Permitted in one step: "Hold the lever and push the button." (same time)
- 5.2 — Permitted in one step, two sentences: "Hold the valve open. The pressure must not be more than 5 bar."
- 5.3 — "The operator should set the switch to OFF" → "Set the switch to OFF."
- 5.3 — "You must now restart the service" → "Start the service again."
- 5.4 — "If the light comes on, stop the pump." "To release the lock, turn the handle."
- 5.5 — Not STE: "NOTE: Clean the surface before you apply the sealant." That is an instruction: make it a step. STE: "NOTE: The sealant becomes hard in 30 minutes." A note can state a result.
- 5.5 — If the information in a note prevents damage or injury, it is a safety instruction, not a note.
- 5.5 — Test: read the procedure without the notes. The reader must still do the task correctly.
- A good procedure layout: a numbered step for each action; the result of an action ("The green light comes on.") on a separate line; sub-steps (a, b, c) when one task has parts.

## 6. Descriptive writing

- Descriptive text has no commands. A product description, a report, and a note in a procedure are descriptive text.
- 6.2 — Use the same key words again in the sentences that follow. A different word for the same thing breaks the connection.
- Not STE: "The cabin pressurization unit, which is fed by two bleed-air ducts that are backed up by a ground connection, supplies the cabin, the cargo bay and the doors, and it is watched by sensors that report to the panel."
- STE: "The pressurization unit supplies air to the cabin, the cargo bay, and the doors. Two bleed-air ducts feed the unit. A ground connection is the backup supply. Sensors monitor the unit and send data to the panel."

## 7. Safety instructions

- WARNING = risk of injury or death. CAUTION = risk of damage to objects (machines, tools, equipment). If both risks are there together, use WARNING. This skill also reads loss of data as damage (CAUTION); that is its own reading, and the author must check it against their standard.
- Other industries use other words (DANGER, ATTENTION, NOTICE; see ANSI Z535 and ISO 3864). Use the standard of the user, and still obey rules 7.1 to 7.3.
- Position: STE gives no rule. Placing a warning before its step is the usual convention of technical publication standards, and this skill follows it as practice.
- If a condition must be true before the reader continues, put the condition first.
- Not STE: "WARNING: Extreme care should be exercised, as the fluid is hot."
- STE: "WARNING: DO NOT TOUCH THE FLUID. THE FLUID IS HOT AND CAN CAUSE BURNS."
- STE: "CAUTION: MAKE SURE THAT YOU HAVE A BACKUP BEFORE YOU DELETE THE DATABASE. YOU CANNOT GET THE DATA BACK."
- Capital letters for the full text are a convention, not a rule. Follow the user's style.

## 8. Punctuation and word counts

- 8.5 — Text in parentheses counts as one word. A parenthesis that is a full sentence must obey the length rule itself.
- 8.6 — "Set the PRESSURE SELECTOR to 25 psi" = 5 words: Set, the, to, the label (1), and 25 psi (1).
- 8.4 — With a list, count the lead-in as one sentence, and each list item as one sentence.
- 8.6 — Do not count the numbers that identify steps or paragraphs. A formula counts as one word.
- 8.2 — Hyphens connect: a compound adjective before a noun ("high-pressure chamber"), a letter or number with a noun ("O-ring", "3-prong connector"), a verb with a first part ("heat-treat"), and a prefix before a vowel ("de-icing").
- For software text, code in backticks, a file path, a URL, and a UI label each count as one word (a convention of this skill).

## 9. Writing practices

- 9.1 — "Care must be exercised when handling the unit" → "Be careful when you move the unit."
- 9.1 — A word-for-word replacement is correct only if the meaning stays the same. "Just apply light pressure" → "Only apply light pressure".
- 9.2 — "The lamp turns red." → "The color of the lamp changes to red." "See if the connection is tight" → "Make sure that the connection is tight."
- 9.3 — *Look into* the fault → "Examine the fault." *Break down* the unit → "Disassemble the unit." *Set up* the test → "Prepare the test." *Go on* → "Continue". Approved two-word verbs with a restricted meaning: MAKE SURE, PUT ON, COME ON.
- 9.4 — If step 1 says "Push the START button", step 9 does not say "Press Start".

## 10. General recommendations (GR)

- GR-1 — "Make sure that the door is locked." (related rule 4.2)
- GR-2 — "With pressure supplied, …" → "When you supply pressure, …". Keep the action verb: "Attach the cover with the four screws". (related rule 1.3)
- GR-4 — If "this" can refer to more than one thing, give the noun or the condition again. (related rule 4.1)
- GR-5 — *actual* ≠ current, *eventually* ≠ possibly, *control* ≠ check. (related rule 1.3)
- GR-6 — *Via* and *vs.* are not approved words. Write "through" and "compared with". (related rule 1.1)
- GR-7 — "He", "she", "man", and "woman" are not permitted unless a context (for example, a medical text) makes them necessary. (related rule 1.1)
- GR-8 — "the manufacturer's instructions" is permitted. If not sure, use "the cover of the pump". (related rule 1.1)
