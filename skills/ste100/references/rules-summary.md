# ASD-STE100 rules: one line each (Issue 9)

Read this file for a Check or Edit job. For examples and the reason behind a rule,
open `rules-details.md` at the section of the rule. The summaries are our own words.
The wording was compared with Issue 9 (2025-01-15). TN = technical noun, TV = technical verb.

## 1. Words

- **1.1** Every word is approved in the dictionary, or is a technical noun or technical verb.
- **1.2** Use an approved word only as the part of speech that the dictionary gives.
- **1.3** Use an approved word only with its approved meaning.
- **1.4** Use verbs and adjectives only in the forms that the dictionary lists.
- **1.5** A word is a TN only if it fits one of the 22 technical noun categories.
- **1.6** An unapproved word can appear only as part of a technical noun.
- **1.7** Do not use a technical noun as a verb.
- **1.8** Use the technical nouns that your company, industry, or subject field accepts.
- **1.9** If you need a technical noun, pick a short and clear one.
- **1.10** Do not use local, informal, or jargon names for technical nouns.
- **1.11** Use one technical noun for each item. Do not switch between names.
- **1.12** A verb is a TV only if it fits one of the 4 technical verb categories; an approved verb that gives the same instruction comes first.
- **1.13** Do not use a technical verb as a noun.
- **1.14** Use American English spelling unless an official directive says otherwise.

## 2. Multi-word nouns

- **2.1** A multi-word noun has three words at most. Nouns and adjectives count; a hyphenated group counts as one word.
- **2.2** Write a longer technical name out in full once; after that, use a shorter form.

## 3. Verbs

- **3.1** Use a verb form only if the dictionary lists it.
- **3.2** Use only the infinitive, the imperative, the simple present, the simple past, and the simple future.
- **3.3** Use the past participle only as an adjective: before a noun, or after BE, BECOME, or STAY.
- **3.4** Do not use helping verbs for complex verb constructions (perfect, progressive, "would have been").
- **3.5** An *-ing* form is allowed only as a technical noun, or as a modifier of one.
- **3.6** Write in the active voice; in a description, use the passive only when the doer is unknown.
- **3.7** Name each action with a verb. Do not turn a noun into the action.

## 4. Sentences

- **4.1** Keep sentences short and clear. Give the action, not an abstract statement.
- **4.2** Keep every needed word, and write no contractions.
- **4.3** Use a vertical list when a sentence holds many items or actions.
- **4.4** Use connecting words to show the logic between related sentences.
- **4.5** Put an article (the, a, an) or a demonstrative (this, these) before a noun where one applies.

## 5. Procedures

- **5.1** A procedure sentence has 20 words at most. A safety instruction obeys this limit too.
- **5.2** One sentence gives one instruction; two actions share a sentence only when they happen at the same time.
- **5.3** Give each instruction as a command. Do not put MUST before a command, unless a safety point needs it.
- **5.4** If an instruction starts with a condition or a purpose, put a comma after it, then the command.
- **5.5** A note gives information only: no instructions, requirements, or limits.

## 6. Descriptive writing

- **6.1** Introduce one new point at a time, after the known information.
- **6.2** Use key words and connecting phrases to build a logical order.
- **6.3** A description sentence has 25 words at most.
- **6.4** Group related information into paragraphs, each starting with a topic sentence.
- **6.5** Each paragraph has only one topic.
- **6.6** A paragraph has six sentences at most.

## 7. Safety instructions

- **7.1** Show the level of risk with a signal word, such as WARNING or CAUTION.
- **7.2** Start each safety instruction with a precise command or a condition.
- **7.3** Explain the risk or the possible result.

## 8. Punctuation and word counts

- **8.1** Standard punctuation is allowed. The semicolon is not.
- **8.2** Use hyphens to join words that are directly related.
- **8.3** Parentheses are allowed for references, item numbers, step identifiers, abbreviations, and explanations.
- **8.4** In a vertical list, a colon counts like a full stop in the word count.
- **8.5** Text in parentheses counts as one word.
- **8.6** A number, a number with its unit, an abbreviation, an identifier, quoted text, a title or a label, and a proper noun each count as one word.
- **8.7** A hyphenated word counts as one word.

## 9. Writing practices

- **9.1** Rewrite the sentence when replacing one word does not work.
- **9.2** Use each approved word only in its approved sense and part of speech.
- **9.3** Do not make a new phrasal verb from two approved words.
- **9.4** Keep terms and wording consistent throughout the text.

## 10. General recommendations (not rules; each gives the nearest related rule)

- **GR-1** Keep "that" after verbs such as "make sure", "show", and "recommend". (related rule 4.2)
- **GR-2** Read "with" again: it can mean "that has", "together with", or "by means of". Write a condition as a condition. (related rule 1.3)
- **GR-3** Use only the pronouns in the dictionary, and only when the noun they refer to is clear. (related rule 4.1)
- **GR-4** Make sure the reader knows what "this" refers to; repeat the noun if there is doubt. (related rule 4.1)
- **GR-5** Watch false friends: words that look like another language's word but mean something else. (related rule 1.3)
- **GR-6** Do not use e.g., i.e., etc.; write "for example", "that is", "and other ...". Also write "through" for *via* and "compared with" for *vs.*. (related rule 1.1)
- **GR-7** Use gender-neutral words; "he", "she", "man", and "woman" are not permitted unless the context needs them. (related rule 1.1)
- **GR-8** The possessive ('s) is permitted when it is clear; otherwise use "of". (related rule 1.1)
